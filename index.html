Introducing Open‑PMC-2M: a curated medical image–text dataset of 2.2M pairs with subfigures, enriched in‑text references, and modality labels.

Abstract

Despite the growing scale of medical Vision‑Language datasets, the impact of dataset quality on model performance remains under‑explored. We introduce Open‑PMC, a high‑quality medical dataset from PubMed Central, containing 2.2 million image‑text pairs, enriched with image modality annotations, subfigures, and summarized in‑text references. The in‑text references provide richer medical context than captions alone. We benchmark Open‑PMC against larger datasets across retrieval and zero‑shot classification tasks, showing that data quality—not just size—drives strong gains. We include feature‑space analysis and release data, trained models, and code.

Overview

We study how curation quality versus quantity affects learned representations in medical vision‑language models. We replace compound figures with subfigures, segment captions, and summarize in‑text references to supply richer supervision. We evaluate encoders across radiology, microscopy, and visible‑light tasks.

Contributions

  • Open‑PMC: 2.2M curated pairs from PMC‑OA with subfigures, subcaptions, and summarized in‑text references; modality labels across Radiology, Microscopy, and VLP.
  • Quality > Quantity: Comparable or better downstream retrieval and zero‑shot results than much larger datasets (e.g., PMC‑15M, Biomedica).
  • Analysis: Feature‑space comparisons (e.g., MMD, t‑SNE) show distinct representation structure learned from Open‑PMC.

Highlights

  • Retrieval: Best Average Recall (AR) and Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR) across benchmarks (MIMIC‑CXR, Quilt, DeepEyeNet).
  • Zero‑shot: Highest mean F1 in radiology; competitive in microscopy and VLP with low variance.
  • Curation Pipeline: DETR‑based subfigure extraction, DocFigure‑based filtering, GPT‑4o‑assisted text segmentation and modality assignment with human validation.

See Sections 3–4 of the paper for full details.

Dataset

Composition

  • 2.2M image–text pairs after rigorous filtering.
  • Images are subfigures from compound figures.
  • Texts include captions, subcaptions, and summarized in‑text references.
  • Modalities: Radiology, Microscopy, Visible‑Light Photography (VLP).

Curation Steps (brief)

  1. Process PMC‑OA (4M+ articles as of 2024‑06‑18) to extract figures/captions/in‑text refs.
  2. DETR subfigure decomposition; DocFigure filtering of non‑medical images.
  3. Segment subcaptions and align to subfigures; summarize in‑text references.
  4. Assign modalities; human validation sample shows ~87% agreement.

Results (at a glance)

  • Retrieval (Recall@200): Open‑PMC encoders achieve the best AR and MRR across Quilt, MIMIC‑CXR, and DeepEyeNet.
  • Zero‑shot classification: Highest mean F1 in radiology; competitive across microscopy and VLP.
  • Representation analysis: Distinct latent structure vs. PMC‑15M and Biomedica (MMD test; t‑SNE visuals).

Refer to Table 2 and Figures 2–3 in the paper for full metrics and plots.

Why it matters

In medical AI, scale alone is not enough. Careful curation—subfigure extraction, richer text context, and modality labeling—produces stronger, more reliable representations that transfer better to real tasks.
